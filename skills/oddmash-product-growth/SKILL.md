# Oddmash product growth

Start with the product's intended audience, current adoption evidence, and what useful behavior means. Do not equate impressions, stars, or downloads with successful use.

- Define a small measurable funnel appropriate to the product: discovery, first useful action, repeated use, and feedback. Establish the baseline and metric definition; label unavailable data rather than inventing a number.
- Collect permitted feedback from issues, discussions, support, or aggregate product data. Group by user problem, frequency, impact, and confidence; distinguish a single request from a repeated pattern. Keep personal data out of public summaries.
- Prioritize experiments by expected user value, evidence, effort, risk, and reversibility. Write the hypothesis, intervention, metric, time window, and stop condition before running it.
- For instrumentation, collect only what is necessary and respect consent, privacy, and product constraints. Do not introduce analytics, tracking, outreach, or paid campaigns without authorization.
- Review outcomes against the baseline, note limitations and confounders, and decide whether to keep, change, or stop the experiment. Convert evidence into a focused backlog with acceptance criteria.
