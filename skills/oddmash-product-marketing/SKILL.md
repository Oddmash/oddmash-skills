# Oddmash product marketing

Read the current product brief, README, brand direction, and actual product. Separate the person who discovers a product from the person who decides to adopt it.

- Define the intended audience, the problem they recognize, the product's differentiated benefit, and one next action. For developer tools, demonstrate a real workflow with a reproducible example; for games, show actual gameplay or a labeled prototype.
- Build a small messaging hierarchy: one promise, concrete evidence, relevant limitations, and a call to action. Keep examples, screenshots, performance claims, compatibility, licensing, and availability aligned with the current release. Do not invent testimonials or benchmark results.
- Match channel intent. Search-oriented pages answer the user's query and use accurate titles, descriptions, headings, and links. Community posts lead with a useful example or discussion; release announcements explain what changed and how to try it. Avoid duplicate outreach and engagement bait.
- Adapt copy for supported languages rather than translating idioms literally. Keep product names, code, links, and claims consistent; public issue and PR communication remains English.
- Produce a reviewable content plan with audience, channel, asset, CTA, and measurement. Distinguish draft content from published content. Obtain authorization before posting, sending outreach, paying, or changing live pages.
